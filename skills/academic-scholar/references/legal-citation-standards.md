# OSCOLA Legal Citation & Primary Authority Verification Standards

> [!NOTE]
> This reference is terminal (1-level deep from entrypoint). Do not link to other markdown reference files.

This reference provides rigorous rules for citing and verifying primary legal authorities under the Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities (OSCOLA), focusing on United Kingdom mental health legislation, common law, and European human rights jurisprudence.

---

## 1. OSCOLA Citation Principles

OSCOLA requires clear, unpunctuated citations designed for academic law writing. Punctuation is minimal: full stops are avoided except at the end of footnotes.

---

## 2. Primary United Kingdom Legislation

### Primary Statutes (Acts of Parliament)
- **Format**: Short title and year without an intervening comma:
  - `Mental Health Act 1983` (MHA 1983)
  - `Mental Capacity Act 2005` (MCA 2005)
  - `Human Rights Act 1998` (HRA 1998)
  - `Health and Social Care Act 2012`
- **Sections and Subsections**:
  - In footnotes, abbreviate section as `s` and sections as `ss`:
    - `Mental Health Act 1983, s 3`
    - `Mental Capacity Act 2005, ss 1-4`
  - In text, spell out the word: "section 3 of the Mental Health Act 1983".
  - Subsections use brackets without spaces: `Mental Capacity Act 2005, s 2(1)`.

### Statutory Instruments (Regulations & Rules)
- **Format**: Name, year, and SI number:
  - `Mental Health (Hospital, Guardianship and Treatment) (England) Regulations 2008, SI 2008/1184`

---

## 3. United Kingdom Case Law

### Post-2001 Decisions (Neutral Citations)
- **Format**: Case name in italics, neutral citation year in square brackets, court abbreviation, judgment number, followed by official Law Reports reference where available:
  - *Cheshire West and Chester Council v P* [2014] UKSC 19, [2014] AC 896
  - *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, [2015] AC 1430
  - *Airedale NHS Trust v Bland* [1993] AC 789 (HL)

### Historical Precedents & Clinical Negligence Standards
- *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582 (QBD)
- *Bolitho v City and Hackney Health Authority* [1998] AC 232 (HL)

---

## 4. Strasbourg & European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) Jurisprudence

### Convention Provisions
- European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):
  - **Article 5**: Right to liberty and security:
    - Article 5(1)(e): Lawful detention of persons of unsound mind.
    - Article 5(4): Entitlement to speedy judicial proceedings to determine detention lawfulness.
  - **Article 8**: Right to respect for private and family life, home, and correspondence:
    - Autonomy, bodily integrity, and non-consensual medical intervention.

### ECtHR Judgments
- **Format**: Case name in italics, application number, judgment date, followed by Reports of Judgments and Decisions (ECHR) or European Human Rights Reports (EHRR):
  - *Winterwerp v Netherlands* (1979) 2 EHRR 387 (minimum standards for lawful psychiatric detention)
  - *HL v United Kingdom* (2005) 40 EHRR 32 (the "Bournewood gap" and deprivation of liberty)
  - *Herczegfalvy v Austria* (1993) 15 EHRR 437 (medical necessity and psychiatric treatment)

## 5. Verification Checklist for Supervisory Audits

1. **Hierarchy of Authority**: Are statutory arguments rooted directly in primary legislation (MHA 1983 or MCA 2005) rather than secondary commentary?
2. **Precision of Section Citations**: Does the citation pinpoint the exact operative subsection (e.g. MCA 2005 s 1(2) presumption of capacity)?
3. **Law Reports Order**: Does the citation prioritize the official Law Reports (AC, QB, Fam, Ch) over unofficial series (WLR, FLR)?
4. **Strasbourg Alignment**: When arguing deprivation of liberty or arbitrary detention, is the *Winterwerp* criteria (objective medical evidence, true mental disorder, persistence) correctly applied?
